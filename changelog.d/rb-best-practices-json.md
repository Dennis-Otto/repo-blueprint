### Features

- **Every repository proposes its answers of OpenSSF Best Practices and of the OSPS Baseline:** the badge of bestpractices.dev asks about 200 questions of each project, which a maintainer answered by hand, and the three levels of the OSPS Baseline started at 0 % even where the blueprint meets most of their criteria. Now every repository gets a `.bestpractices.json`, which bestpractices.dev reads when you add the project and when you press *Save (and continue) 🤖* on a level: 90 to 100 answers of the levels passing and silver and of the Baseline, each with the file of the repository that shows it, such as the ruleset of `main`, the private vulnerability reporting, the signed releases with their SBOMs and OpenVEX, the DCO, the dependency updates and CodeQL. It answers only what the blueprint guarantees for the kind of project and your answers of Copier, such as a designated successor or a website on GitHub Pages; what only your software and your people can say, such as its cryptography, its user guides or the review by a second person, stays yours to answer on bestpractices.dev, which keeps those answers. `checklist` now shows the badges of both series and, once one of them names the number of the project, the form of the level to fill in and the Markdown of the badge that is missing. `SECURITY.md` says that nobody can push to `main` directly, force-push it or delete it.
